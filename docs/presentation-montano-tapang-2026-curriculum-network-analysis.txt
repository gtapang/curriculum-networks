So here’s what we did in our study. This research employs a dual-network analysis, looking at both the intended curriculum checklist vis-à-vis the actual student progression within the curriculum. 

First, we modeled the formal 156-unit hospitality curriculum as a directed graph to quantify its inherent complexity. Following Yang et al. (2024), the curriculum is formally encoded as the Course Prerequisite Network, where a graph is represented by vectors and edges. This means the courses with their prerequisites are linked together as edges. Courses that are corequisites are also linked together, but given a lower weight.


Next, we modeled the students’ actual academic histories to identify deviations from the theoretical blueprint. We extracted and anonymized 269 curriculum checklists of currently enrolled students. Here, we map the subjects students actually took sequentially, including retakes. Each node was enriched with performance data, including total attempts, failure rate, counts of incomplete, and dropped attempts. Edges were weighted by the volume of student flow between subjects across all recorded semesters.

We used Gephi to visualize the two networks. The ForceAtlas2 force-directed algorithm was used (Jacomy et al., 2014), which positions high-degree hub nodes centrally and clusters tightly-connected courses together. Community detection was performed using the Louvain algorithm (Blondel et al., 2008), and modularity scores (Newman & Girvan, 2004) were computed for both networks. We also used Sankeymatic to map out students’ current standing based on their milestones in the curriculum checklist.

This is our Empirical Progression Network, mapping 1,621 active transition edges. The network reorganizes into three functional layers. Layer 1 consists of foundational launchpads like Macro-perspectives and Accounting. 

But look closely at Layer 2, the blue nodes. Here sits CALC, emerging as our dominant academic bottleneck. It recorded 295 attempts from 194 unique students. It holds an alarming 37.3% failure rate. The thick self-loop edge on Calculus has a weight of 71, meaning we have 71 instances of students being forced to re-enroll in the exact same course after failing. Statistics follows a parallel, though less severe, bottleneck path with a 13.2% failure rate and 21 retake self-loops
